THE FOUNDING STORY

Before there was ReLeaf,
there was a mother’s question.

As a mother, Atika Yakubu saw used papers and exercise books coming home with her children. Term after term, the pages accumulated.

She imagined an organised way to recover that paper—and explore how its value could find a way back to education.

That everyday observation became the starting point for ReLeaf Collective: a Ghanaian initiative developing connections between schools, responsible recovery and useful new materials.

A MATERIAL CONNECTION. A HUMAN PURPOSE.

More than
what paper becomes.

Recovery is one part of the idea. Keeping a connection to education is what gives it purpose.

We want to bring schools, recovery specialists and manufacturing partners into a shared conversation. Together, we can explore useful next uses for paper and a practical way for value to return to learning.

WHAT GUIDES US

every connection.

The principles we want to build into the work.

01

Start with people.

Listen to schools and learn from the expertise already in the recovery chain.

02

Make the terms clear.

Agree permissions, suitable materials and any school benefit before a pilot begins.

03

Let evidence lead.

Be clear about what we’re developing. Publish results when they can be verified.
